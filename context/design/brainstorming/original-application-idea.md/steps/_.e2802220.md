# Original application idea for ArcHive, to potentially use as context

## Problem Statement

***The Domain: Daily Routines***

I have a very extensive daily routine that I hold myself to, which include chores like grocery shopping and laundry, self-reflective journaling twice a day, and health-related goals like going to the gym and drinking a cup of water every hour. I constantly plan and write lists of things I should do daily, mapped out hour by hour, especially when I learn of something that I plan to make a habit out of.

***The Problem***

Oftentimes, when I am unable to stick to my planned routine, whether due to forgetfulness or laziness, I end up giving up on good habits that I try to create, and waste much more time than I planned for. This leads to a cycle of feeling increasingly unmotivated and stressed as days pass. Many people who attempt to establish daily routines face a similar issue with motivation. Individual goals are often difficult to attain without external support from one's social circle, which allows a goal to shift from just a wish in one's mind to something tangible, grounded, and achievable. A lack of such support may be one of the key reasons motivation falters.

***Stakeholders***

* User (the individual trying to build a habit or routine)<br>

Role: The user wants to gain consistency and structure in their routine and motivation from peers to continue steadily with progress through friendly peer-pressure.

* Peer group (other people with a shared goal who encourage consistency)<br>

Role: Peers want to benefit from shared accountability and bond over shared goals, leading to more emotional closeness within the group.

* Potential users (those that are invited to share a goal, or those that want to share a goal with a peer group but cannot due to having a lack of social connections)<br>

Role: Some people would like to keep their individual goals private, and would thus feel unnecessary peer pressure from others to join them, while others may feel shame for not having a group that they are close enough to for progress sharing.

***Evidence***

**Abandonment of goals is common**<br>

Only about 10% of people actually succeed in keeping their New Year's resolutions. [Link](https://dreammakerr.com/new-years-resolutions-statistics/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

**Improving self-efficacy through peer support**<br>

A meta-analysis found that peer support significantly improved both self-efficacy (ability to do things) and self-management behaviors for people with type 2 diabetes. This suggests peer involvement helps people stick with changes. [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33229189/)

**Sustaining habits through peer encouragement**<br>

In a study of ~2,200 adolescents, peer support had a strong effect on exercise adherence. This shows peer encouragement helps sustain habits. [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36832530/)

**Peer pressure is effective in driving change**<br>

Using a "go with the crowd" mentality causes children to tend to give in to peer pressure, which can be repurposed to help them achieve their goals. [Link](https://centerstone.org/our-resources/health-wellness/what-is-peer-pressure-and-who-is-at-risk/)

**Peer support apps are already being used in the mental health domain due to their effectiveness**<br>

Many peer support apps exist since they are effective in helping people overcome mental health issues (users reported that they understood the value of socializing in those settings and felt more heard), and the same group support may help individuals achieve their goals and establish healthy routines. [Link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39051360/)

**Limited social sharing in existing activity trackers**<br>

Many mood/activity trackers are mostly made for individual use and have limited social sharing. [Link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daylio)

## Pitch: 🐝~ArcHive

Many people struggle to stick to routines and build habits, often losing motivation and giving up despite their best intentions. **ArcHive** solves this by making habit formation social, engaging, and rewarding, so users can consistently make progress toward their goals.

The name ArcHive evokes both a personal journey of growth—an “arc”—and a cozy, collaborative community, like a hive of bees working together. The app serves as an archive of progress, encouraging users to take ownership of their routines and individual growth while simultaneously strengthening their bonds with peers who support their growth.

**Key Features**:

1. **Stat Radar** – Users track core aspects of their lives through attributes like that of a video game character: HP (to measure diet, hydration), energy/stamina (to track sleep/restedness), physical strength/power (to track fitness/resilience/endurance), agility/dexterity (to track productivity, creativity, skill performance), and intelligence (to track skill mastery, and learning). These would be displayed on a dynamic radar chart on the user's profile. Daily contributions to habits automatically update these stats, giving a visual and motivating representation of progress. This helps users see the impact of their actions and encourages consistency.

2. **Arcs & Hive Collaboration** – Users create “arcs,” habit trackers centered around a stat, and can invite friends using friend codes. For group arcs, a day of progress only counts if everyone completes their tasks, fostering accountability and peer motivation. This social system encourages users to stay on track, knowing their peers rely on them.

3. **Gamification & Rewards** – Completing tasks daily and maintaining streaks earns avatar points, which can be spent in a gacha-style system to unlock collectible avatars of varying rarity. The user's distribution of stats that they have contributed to determine what avatars are available to them in the gacha system. This adds a fun, game-like element that turns habit-building into a rewarding experience, keeping users engaged over time.

By combining visual progress tracking, social accountability, and gamified rewards, ArcHive transforms habit formation from a solitary struggle into a collaborative and enjoyable journey, motivating users to consistently pursue their goals.

## Concept Design

**Concepts**

```

concept Authentication

purpose authenticate users so that each user of the app is a real person

principle a user is authorized to access their profile only if they provide the correct username and password set during registration

state

a set of Users with

a Username String

a Password String

actions

register (username, password: String): (user: User)

requires no user exists with given username

effect creates and stores a new User with the given username and password, returns the new user

  

authenticate (username, password: String): (user: User)

requires a user exists with the given username

effect returns the user if the password matches, otherwise fails

```

```

concept Friending

purpose allow users to easily access each others profiles for mutual accountability

principle users have automatically generated friend codes, and when another user adds their friend code, both users will be able able to see each other in their hives (friend list).

state

a set of Friendships with

a UserA User

a UserB User

  

a set of UsersToFriendCode with

a Username String

a FriendCode String

actions

generateFriendCode (user: User): (friendCode: String)

requires user exists and does not already have an assigned FriendCode

effect randomly generates a unique friend code and assigns it to the user, and returns the friend code

  

getUserByFriendCode (friendCode: String): (user: User)

requires friendCode exists

effect returns user associated with friendCode

  

addFriend (from: User, to: User): (friendship: Friendship)

requires from ≠ to and no existing friendship between from and to

effect creates a mutual friendship

  

listFriends (user: User): (friends: Set of User)

requires user exists

effect returns all users in a Friendship pair with that user

```

```

concept StatTracking[User]

purpose represent a user’s personal growth as video game–like stats

principle each completed task from a user's arc contributes points to one stat; the number of completed tasks corresponding to a stat will be displayed in the stat's progress bar, out of the number of total tasks for that stat. This way, uncompleted tasks are visible in the stat's progress bar.

state

a set of Stats with

a user User

a pair of numbers HP (CompletedNumber, IncompletedNumber)

a pair of numbers Stamina (CompletedNumber, IncompletedNumber)

a pair of numbers Strength (CompletedNumber, IncompletedNumber)

a pair of numbers Agility (CompletedNumber, IncompletedNumber)

a pair of numbers Intelligence (CompletedNumber, IncompletedNumber)

actions

updateStatWithCompletedTask (user: User, stat: String, delta: Number): (updatedStats: Stats)

requires user exists and stat ∈ {HP, Stamina, Strength, Agility, Intelligence}

effect increases user’s stat's CompletedNumber by delta

  

updateStatWithIncompleteTask (user: User, stat: String, delta: Number): (updatedStats: Stats)

requires user exists and stat ∈ {HP, Stamina, Strength, Agility, Intelligence}

effect increases user’s stat's IncompletedNumber by delta

  

getStats (user: User): (stats: Stats)

requires user exists

effect returns the current stat values for the user

  

initializeStats (user: User): (stats: Stats)

requires user exists

effect assigns user to a new set of Stats and returns stats with all numbers initialized to 0

```

```

concept ArcTracking

purpose allow users to create and manage arcs (habit trackers) either individually or with any number of peers

principle an arc consists of a daily habit or set of tasks; progress counts only if completed, and in group arcs, progress counts only if all members complete their tasks

state

a set of Arcs with

a Name String

a Stat String {HP, Stamina, Strength, Agility, Intelligence}

a Members Set of Users

a Streak Number

a Progress Map<User, Boolean>

actions

createArc (name: String, members: Set of Users): (arc: Arc)

requires all users in members have less than 10 arcs that they are a member of

effect creates a new arc with given name and stat, and adds the current user and members to Members and to the progress map with initial progress set to false and initial streak set to 0; members may be empty

  

addMemberToArc (user: User, arc: Arc)

requires user exists and arc exists

effect adds user to arc’s Members and progress map with initial progress set to false

  

markProgress (user: User, arc: Arc): (progress: Progress Map<User, Boolean>)

requires user ∈ arc.Members

effect sets user’s progress for the day to true

  

markNoProgress (user: User, arc: Arc): (progress: Progress Map<User, Boolean>)

requires user ∈ arc.Members

effect sets user's progress for the day to false

  

getArcStatus (arc: Arc): (status: Map<User, Boolean>)

requires arc exists

effect returns current progress status of all members

  

updateArcStreak (arc: Arc): (newStreak: Number)

requires arc exists

effect gets the progress map of the arc, and if any member has not made progress, the streak resets to 0, otherwise it is incremented by 1

  

getArcs (user: User): (arcs: Set of Arcs)

requires user exists

effect returns all arcs that the user is a member of

```

```

concept Rewarding

purpose motivate users by granting rewards (avatars) for consistent habit completion and progress

principle users earn avatar points by completing arcs and maintaining streaks; points are spent in a gacha system that yields avatars based on user stat distribution

state

a set of Rewards with

a User User

Points Number

Avatars Set of Avatar

  

a set of Avatars with

a Name String

a Rarity {common, rare, epic, legendary}

a StatAffinity Map<Stat, Number>

actions

initializeRewards (user: User)

requires user exists

effect add user to Rewards with Points initialized to 0 and with no avatars

  

earnPoints (user: User, points: Number)

requires user exists

effect increases user’s point balance

  

spendPoints (user: User, cost: Number): (avatar: Avatar)

requires user exists and user.Points ≥ cost

effect deducts points and randomly assigns avatar by rarity; the set of avatars available is from getAvailableAvatars; adds avatar to user’s Avatars

  

listAvatars (user: User): (avatars: Set of Avatar)

effect returns all avatars owned by user

  

getAvailableAvatars (user: User): (avatars: Set of Avatar)

requires user exists

effect for each avatar that exists, add it to the set of avatars to be returned if the user's stats indicate a completed number greater than or equal to the number in the corresponding StatAffinity map for each stat in that map; this indicates that the user has met the requirements to unlock that avatar

```

**Syncs**

```

sync createUser

when Authentication.register (username, password): (user: User)

then Friending.generateFriendCode (user)

Rewarding.initializeRewards (user)

StatTracking.initializeStats (user)

  

sync arcProgressToStats

when ArcTracking.markProgress (user, arc)

then updateStatWithCompletedTask(user: user, stat: arc.associatedStat, delta: 1)

  

sync incompleteArcToStats

when ArcTracking.markNoProgress (user, arc)

then updateStatWithIncompletedTask(user: user, stat: arc.associatedStat, delta: 1)

  

sync arcProgressToReward

when ArcTracking.getArcStatus (arc): (status)

then if all status[user] = true for user in arc.Members

for each user in arc.Members

Rewarding.earnPoints (user: user, points: 1)

Rewarding.earnPoints (user: user, points: arc.streak)

```

**Notes**

* Authentication must occur in order for a user profile to be created.

* Friending allows participation in group arcs, shared stat tracker visibility, and shared reward visibility.

* The user's profile is made up of their stats (one instance of StatTracking per user), a list of accumulated rewards and an active avatar, a friend list (hive), and a list of ArcTracking instances.

* Rewards depend on all members of an arc completing their daily task, but stats depend on individual completion.

## User Journey

Alex is a busy college student who has tried countless times to stick to healthier routines—morning exercise, drinking water every hour, and journaling twice a day. At first, Alex writes out detailed lists of tasks, but when the demands of classes and social life intrude, the routine quickly falls apart. After missing just a few days, Alex feels discouraged and unmotivated, abandoning the routine altogether. This cycle repeats again and again.

One evening, after yet another unproductive day, Alex’s roommate suggests trying ArcHive, and Alex signs up. Alex plans to assign his habits to video game–like stats by creating an arc. For example, drinking water contributes to HP, daily workouts add to Strength, and journaling adds to Stamina. Alex creates a new arc: “Daily Gym.” This arc is tied to the Strength stat. To stay accountable, Alex adds his roommate to this arc after adding his friend code, since he is also interested in going to the gym daily. Now, the progress for this arc only counts if both of them complete their gym session each day. Alex feels more motivated knowing that missing a day would break the streak not just for them, but for the whole group.

The next morning, Alex checks in at the gym and marks the arc task complete. ArcHive syncs this progress to Alex’s Strength stat, visibly filling the radar chart. The visualization immediately feels motivating—each habit is no longer just a task, but part of Alex’s overall “character growth.” Later, his roommate also completes the arc task. Since everyone in the arc completed their workout, the app updates their streak and rewards each of them with avatar points. After a few days, the two rack up points quickly due to their sustained streak. Alex spends the points in the gacha and unlocks a rare avatar, themed around strength. The small win feels exciting and playful—like progress in a game.

Over time, Alex begins to look forward to checking the app daily. The stat radar provides a clear visual of balance: too much focus on Strength makes Intelligence and Stamina lag behind, nudging Alex to journal more and get enough sleep. By reframing routine-building as a collaborative, gamified journey, ArcHive helps Alex sustain habits that once felt impossible. Instead of dreading missed days, Alex now feels supported, accountable, and motivated to keep growing—both for themselves and their hive.
